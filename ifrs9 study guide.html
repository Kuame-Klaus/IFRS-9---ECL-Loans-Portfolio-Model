Sheet 01

The three pillars of IFRS 9

IFRS 9 replaced IAS 39 in three separate blocks. Most of the day-to-day work — and almost all of the ECL modelling you've been building — sits in the second one.

1. Classification & Measurement

Determines whether a financial asset is measured at amortised cost, fair value through OCI, or fair value through P&L — based on the business model and the asset's cash flow characteristics.

2. Impairment (ECL)

Replaces IAS 39's "incurred loss" model with a forward-looking "expected credit loss" model — losses are provisioned before a default event actually happens. This is the engine behind the loan portfolio ECL build.

3. Hedge Accounting

A more principles-based, less mechanical model for aligning hedge accounting with an entity's actual risk management activity.

Key idea: under IAS 39 you provisioned once a loss event had already happened. Under IFRS 9 you provision for losses you expect to happen — which is why ECL allowances jumped materially for most banks on transition.
Sheet 02

Classification: the SPPI test

Every debt financial asset is classified by asking two questions in sequence.

Question 1 — Business model

Is the asset held to collect contractual cash flows, held both to collect and to sell, or held for trading / other purposes?

Question 2 — Cash flow characteristics (SPPI)

Do the contractual cash flows represent Solely Payments of Principal and Interest on the principal outstanding — i.e. does it look like a basic lending arrangement (principal plus compensation for time value of money and credit risk), or does it have equity-like or derivative-like features?

Amortised Cost

Business model: hold to collect. Cash flows: pass SPPI. Most term loans and trade receivables land here.

FVOCI

Business model: hold to collect and sell. Cash flows: pass SPPI. Common for liquidity-portfolio bonds.

FVTPL

Fails SPPI, or held for trading, or business model is neither of the above. Equities and most derivatives default here.

Judgment risk: ECL only applies to amortised cost and FVOCI debt instruments (plus loan commitments and financial guarantees) — not to FVTPL assets, which are already remeasured to fair value through profit or loss. Getting the classification wrong changes whether an asset is in the ECL population at all.
Sheet 03

Staging & significant increase in credit risk (SICR)

This is the model you already built in the loan portfolio workbook — the three-stage flow shown in the hero diagram above.

No significant increase in credit risk since initial recognition. Recognise 12-month ECL — the portion of lifetime losses expected from default events possible within 12 months. Interest revenue is calculated on the gross carrying amount.
SICR has occurred since origination but the asset isn't yet credit-impaired. Recognise lifetime ECL. Common SICR triggers: DPD >= 30 (the standard's rebuttable presumption), internal rating downgrade beyond a defined threshold, being placed on a watchlist, or qualitative indicators like covenant breaches. Interest revenue is still on the gross carrying amount.
Objective evidence of impairment / default — DPD >= 90 is the standard's rebuttable presumption of default. Recognise lifetime ECL, PD effectively 100%. Interest revenue switches to being calculated on the net carrying amount (gross carrying amount less the loss allowance).
Note: movement between stages goes both ways — an asset that recovers (cure period met, arrears cleared, rating restored) moves back down a stage, reverting from lifetime to 12-month ECL.
Sheet 04

The ECL formula: PD × LGD × EAD

ECL = Probability of Default × Loss Given Default × Exposure at Default, discounted to present value at the asset's effective interest rate

PD

Likelihood of default within 12 months (Stage 1) or over the remaining life (Stage 2/3). Usually derived from an internal rating grade mapped to a transition matrix, or a scorecard.

LGD

Proportion of exposure not expected to be recovered after default, net of collateral recoveries (discounted for time and haircut for forced-sale illiquidity) and recovery costs.

EAD

Expected outstanding balance at the point of default — outstanding drawn balance plus any undrawn commitment multiplied by a credit conversion factor (CCF).

This is exactly the mechanics behind the Loan Portfolio workbook: Stage drives which PD horizon applies, collateral haircuts drive LGD, and amortised outstanding balance drives EAD.

Sheet 05

Forward-looking information

IFRS 9 (para 5.5.17) requires ECL to reflect reasonable and supportable forward-looking information, not just historical loss experience. In practice this means:

Macroeconomic scenarios

Most models run multiple scenarios (e.g. base, upside, downside — GDP growth, unemployment, commodity prices, exchange rates) and probability-weight the ECL outcome across them rather than using a single deterministic path.

Management overlays

Judgmental adjustments layered on top of the modelled output when the model hasn't yet caught up with an emerging risk (e.g. a new tariff regime or a sector-specific shock) — these need clear documentation and governance sign-off.

Judgment risk: overlays that are large, one-directional (always reducing provisions), or undocumented are a classic red flag — they can become a mechanism for earnings management if not independently challenged.
Sheet 06

Worked example

A Stage 2 loan: outstanding balance (EAD) GHS 1,000,000, lifetime PD 15%, collateral forced-sale value GHS 700,000 with recovery costs of 5%.

Net recoverable = 700,000 × (1 − 0.05) = GHS 665,000
LGD = (1,000,000 − 665,000) / 1,000,000 = 33.5%
ECL (undiscounted) = 0.15 × 0.335 × 1,000,000 = GHS 50,250

If this same loan later hits 90+ days past due and is reclassified to Stage 3, PD jumps to 100% and the calculation shifts to a discounted-cash-flow workout basis rather than the PD × LGD × EAD formula:

ECL (Stage 3) ≈ EAD − PV(expected recoveries) = 1,000,000 − 665,000 = GHS 335,000

The roughly 6.7x jump between the two figures is exactly why the Stage 2 vs Stage 3 boundary is the single most consequential judgment call in the whole model.

Sheet 07

What a reviewer checks first

Loans past 90 DPD retained in Stage 2 without a documented, approved rebuttal are one of the most common findings — the 90-day default presumption can be rebutted, but only with clear evidence, not just left unaddressed.
LGD is only as good as the collateral value feeding it. Check the valuation date, methodology (forced-sale vs market value), and whether haircuts are refreshed periodically rather than fixed at origination.
Revolving or partially-drawn facilities should include an undrawn commitment × CCF component in EAD — omitting this understates exposure and, in turn, ECL.
Management overlays should be documented, approved, time-bound, and reassessed each reporting period — not left as a permanent plug.
The DPD and rating-grade thresholds used to trigger Stage 2 should be applied consistently across the portfolio and periodically back-tested against actual default experience, not set once and never revisited.
Self-check

Five questions before you close the book

1. A bond is held both to collect contractual cash flows and to sell if liquidity is needed, and its cash flows pass the SPPI test. How is it classified?
Fair Value through Other Comprehensive Income (FVOCI). The "hold to collect and sell" business model combined with passing SPPI is the specific combination that lands an asset in FVOCI rather than amortised cost or FVTPL.
2. What's the key difference between 12-month ECL and lifetime ECL?
12-month ECL only captures losses from default events that are possible within the next 12 months (used for Stage 1). Lifetime ECL captures losses from all possible default events over the full remaining life of the instrument (used for Stage 2 and Stage 3). It's not a 12-month vs. full-term loss amount — it's about which default events are being considered, weighted by their probability.
3. A loan is 45 days past due and has had no rating downgrade or other qualitative indicator. What stage is it likely in, and why?
Stage 2. Being 30+ days past due is the standard's rebuttable presumption of a significant increase in credit risk, which moves the asset to lifetime ECL even without any other quantitative or qualitative trigger.
4. Why might two loans with identical PD and EAD have very different ECL?
Because LGD can differ significantly based on collateral type and quality. A loan secured by cash or a low-haircut asset will have a much lower LGD (and therefore lower ECL) than an otherwise identical unsecured loan or one secured by an asset with a high forced-sale haircut, like inventory or specialised equipment.
5. Under IFRS 9, when does interest revenue switch from being calculated on the gross carrying amount to the net carrying amount?
On entering Stage 3 (credit-impaired). In Stage 1 and Stage 2, interest is still calculated on the gross carrying amount; once an asset is credit-impaired, interest revenue is calculated on the net carrying amount — gross carrying amount less the loss allowance.
